Icon Concepts

Four directions for review

Hatchery · Brand Identity · Issue #4

A Geometric Egg

A clean ovoid silhouette — slightly asymmetric top, wider base. Pure shape, no detail. Communicates origin, potential, the brand name directly. Gold fill on light; reversed on dark.

B Cracked Shell

An egg split at the midline — two halves slightly separated, a thin gap between them. Implies emergence, hatching in progress. The crack line is the story. Works as a two-tone mark (gold body, dark gap).

C Dragon Scale / Facet

A single faceted scale — a diamond with a curved bottom edge, suggesting one scale from a reptilian surface. Geometric, distinctive. References the dragon hatchery metaphor without being literal. Multi-facet shading gives depth using gold tones.

D Stylised H

The letter H with the crossbar replaced by an egg form — a horizontal oval sits where the bar would be. The letterform is immediately legible at any size. The egg crossbar is the conceptual hook that ties the mark to the brand. Two verticals in gold, egg crossbar as a lighter or outlined form.

Notes

  • A — Geometric Egg: Strongest brand alignment. The two-ellipse construction gives a natural egg silhouette (wide base tapering to a rounded top). Very clean at small sizes.
  • B — Cracked Shell: Most narrative — implies the action of hatching. The zigzag crack drops out at 16px, so small-size variant would fall back to the solid split form.
  • C — Dragon Scale: Most geometric and distinctive. The facet shading adds depth but can be stripped to a single fill for flat/small contexts. References the hatchery metaphor obliquely.
  • D — Stylised H: Highest legibility at all sizes. The egg crossbar is a subtle nod rather than a primary statement — some viewers will read it as a plain H. Strong for app favicons and UI contexts where the wordmark isn't present.
  • All concepts use #C9A84C as the primary fill. Light/dark background variants are structurally identical — no outline inversion required at this palette.
